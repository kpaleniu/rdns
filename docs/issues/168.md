### 168. A resolution has no deadline: one `resolve` can take 157.5 s, holding a UDP permit throughout — **filed 2026-10-04**, **bug**, **ready-for-agent**

Found grilling #166. Nothing bounds a whole resolution. Each exchange is bounded
at `timeout_ms / 2` (`recurse.rs`'s `exchange_timeout`, 2.5 s at the default
5000), and `ask_any` tries every server of a delegation in turn, spending one of
`query_budget`'s 64 per try. The budget is a count of queries, not of time.

Measured with a throwaway paused-clock probe, reverted: `Resolver::resolve` in
recursive mode, default `ResolverConfig`, a root answering with a referral to
`example.test.` whose glued nameservers never answer.

| nameservers | elapsed |
|---|---|
| 1 | 2.5 s |
| 4 | 10 s |
| 13 | 32.5 s |
| 70 | 157.5 s (63 × 2.5 s: the budget, less the root's) |

Four silent nameservers already reach `--connection-idle-timeout`'s default
10 s. A UDP client has retried or given up long before, so the answer is
computed for nobody. Measured on the resolver alone, not through `rdnsr`'s `handle_query`; `--serve-stale-first`
answers from cache ahead of a refresh and was not part of it.

How the transports bound a slow handler differs: TCP waits for it however long
it takes (`tcp.rs`'s writer drains after the read loop ends); DoQ and DoH close
an idle connection with the answer still in flight one `idle_timeout` later,
so a lone slow query gets about twice the idle timeout (`quic.rs`'s
`serve_connection`, `https.rs`'s `until_idle`). A deadline below the idle
timeout would make the difference moot.

No remedy costed. A deadline value wants checking against BIND's
`resolver-query-timeout` and Unbound before one is chosen (`CLAUDE.md` §4).

**Grilled 2026-10-04.** The cost is not only waste. `rdnsr`'s UDP loop holds
one of `MAX_INFLIGHT_UDP`'s 1024 permits for the whole resolution
(`serve.rs`'s `udp_main`) and drops a datagram arriving with none free. A
throwaway probe there, reverted: 70 silent glued nameservers, `timeout_ms` 200,
one permit; an UPDATE sent every 250 ms got its NOTIMP only after the query's
SERVFAIL, at 6.9 s — 63 exchanges of 100 ms. At the default 2.5 s per
exchange that is the 157.5 s above, so distinct names under one such zone at
1024 / 157.5 s ≈ 6.5 queries a second hold every permit, and every other UDP
client is dropped. Anyone can delegate a zone to addresses that do not answer.

What the others bound, read in their source:

| | whole-resolution limit | on expiry |
|---|---|---|
| BIND `5375ad9` | `resolver-query-timeout`, 10 s, 301 ms–30 s, wall clock per fetch (`lib/dns/resolver.c`, `fctx_expired`) | SERVFAIL, EDE 22, fetch cancelled |
| PowerDNS Recursor `afd6e83` | `max-total-msec`, 7 s, upstream wait summed, checked before each send (`syncres.cc`, `checkTotalTime`) | SERVFAIL, EDE 22, cancelled |
| Knot Resolver 5.7.8, 6.5.0 | `KR_RESOLVE_TIME_LIMIT`, 10 s, compiled in (`lib/defines.h`) | SERVFAIL, EDE 22, cancelled |
| Unbound `eafb027` | none in time; `max-sent-count` 32 and the infra cache's down hosts bound it | — |

RFC 8767 §5 names it: "A query resolution timer, which caps the total amount
of time a recursive resolver spends processing the query. [...] commonly
around 10 to 30 seconds. If this timer expires on an attempted lookup that is
still being processed, the resolution effort is abandoned."

**Brief:**

- A resolution timer in `rdns::Resolver`, beside the query budget, so every
  resolution is bounded: a client's, a prefetch, DNS64's A query, a trust
  anchor refresh. Wall clock from the start of `resolve_validated`, enforced
  by cancelling the future, so it cannot overrun as PowerDNS's summed form can.
- `--resolution-timer` and `resolver.resolution-timer`, seconds, default 10
  (BIND, Knot, and `--connection-idle-timeout`'s default), refused outside
  1–30 (BIND's ceiling and RFC 8767's range).
- Expiry is `ResolveError::ResolutionTimer`, carrying the limit, and the
  client gets SERVFAIL with EDE 22, No Reachable Authority (RFC 8914 §4.23).
  A stale answer, where one is held, is served through the failure path that
  exists (RFC 8767 §4), shown by a test rather than assumed.
- DNS64's A query has its own timer, so a DNS64 client can wait two. Accepted:
  bounded either way, and sharing one would thread a `Resolution` through
  `cached_or_resolve`.

**Not this item:** the timer makes the attack 16 times dearer — 1024 / 10 s
≈ 102 queries a second still holds every permit — and does not close it.
BIND shares one fetch among clients and limits fetches per zone and per
server; Unbound bounds sends per resolution and skips hosts it has found down,
and its `discard-timeout` frees the client while the work goes on. Which of
those, filed on closing this as its own number.
