### 209. The dnstap pump holds no `Busy`, so the drain does not wait for it — **filed 2026-10-10**, **bug**, **closed 2026-10-11**

Seen while working #208. `Sink::spawn` hands the pump `stop` alone and drops
its `JoinHandle` (`rdns-transport/src/dnstap.rs`). `serve_until_stopped`
drains `Busy` claims, then `main` returns and the runtime drops what is left
(`CLAUDE.md` §9: dropping a `JoinHandle` detaches the task). Nothing orders
the pump's last writes, the queued frames and STOP, before that exit.

Provoked on Linux (WSL, Fedora 44, 157cec4): `rdnsd --dnstap file:...`, 50
queries, SIGTERM, 20 times. All 20 captures ended with STOP, exit 0. So for a
file the pump wins the race in practice.

Not tried: a TCP collector, where STOP is followed by waiting for FINISH, a
round trip the drain does not cover; and `rdnsr`, which spawns its sink the
same way. Either could lose the tail of a capture.

If it is real, the pump holding a `Busy` would make the drain wait for it, and
`rdns::test_util::closed_capture`'s polls could await the drain instead.

## Closed (2026-10-11, `c5230e1`)

Real, and the TCP target had a second bug: the pump sent FINISH itself.
Frame Streams has the reader send FINISH in reply to STOP, and the writer
wait for it: `fstrm_capture.c:839` sends it, `writer.c:323` waits for it,
and libfstrm's I/O thread closes through that writer (`iothr.c:453`).

- `Sink::spawn` takes a `Lifecycle`. The pump holds its `Busy` until the
  stream is closed, after the collector's FINISH on a socket. That wait is
  bounded by the drain budget.
- `rdns::test_util::closed_capture` begins the stop and awaits the drain.
  It no longer polls the file.
- The TCP test encoded the bug. It asserted STOP then FINISH from the
  writer; it now answers FINISH as a collector and checks that the drain
  waits for it.
- A process test (unix) runs rdnsd with a TCP collector and sends SIGTERM.
  The collector holds back FINISH, and the connection stays open until it
  arrives. Then rdnsd exits 0.

Provoked in-process with the `Busy` released at once: both file-target
tests lost STOP, and the TCP test's drain ended before FINISH (Windows).
The process test fails the same way on Linux: "closed the stream before
FINISH: Ok(0)". So the 20-of-20 file result was the race won, not a
guarantee. Not tried separately: `rdnsr` as a process. It spawns the same
`Sink` with its own `Lifecycle`, and `tap.rs`'s in-process test now awaits
its drain.
