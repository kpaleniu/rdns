### 169. A black-hole zone still fills `rdnsr`'s UDP permits at ~102 queries a second — **filed 2026-10-04, closed 2026-10-05**, **bug**

Found closing #168. The resolution timer bounds one resolution at 10 s, so a
UDP query for a name under a zone whose nameservers never answer holds one of
`MAX_INFLIGHT_UDP`'s 1024 permits for 10 s (`serve.rs`'s `udp_main`; 10 s is
`a_resolution_is_abandoned_when_its_timer_expires`). Distinct names under one
such zone at 1024 / 10 s ≈ 102 queries a second hold every permit, and
`udp_main` drops every datagram that arrives with none free, from any client.
Before #168 it was ~6.5 a second. Arithmetic on the two measured halves, not
a flood driven end to end. Below any per-source `--query-rate` worth setting,
and spread over sources anyway.

Nothing in `rdnsr` sees that the queries share a zone. What the others have,
read in BIND's ARM at `5375ad9` (`doc/arm/reference.rst`) and in Unbound and
PowerDNS source for #168:

- BIND `recursive-clients`, 1000: over the quota, "for each incoming request
  a previous pending request is dropped" — the oldest gives way, where
  `udp_main` refuses the newest, so a flood cannot hold the slots against a
  new client.
- BIND `fetches-per-zone` and `fetches-per-server`: simultaneous fetches to
  one zone, or one server, before more are dropped or SERVFAILed. Both
  default to 0, off; `fetches-per-server` adjusts itself to congestion.
- BIND `clients-per-query`: clients attached to one fetch for one name and
  type; past it the zone is assumed unresponsive. Only identical questions.
- Unbound `max-sent-count`, 32, sends per resolution; its infra cache marks a
  host down and skips it; `discard-timeout`, 1.9 s, sends no reply to a
  request older than that while the resolution goes on.

`ask_any` tries every server of a delegation on every query: the RTT store
charges a silent one its full timeout, and nothing skips it. No remedy
costed; the shapes differ in what a legitimate client of a slow zone loses,
which is what wants measuring.

**Grilled 2026-10-05.** The default `--query-rate` is 200 a source, so one
unspoofed address is enough; the remedy has to hold against spoofed sources,
which rules out anything keyed per source as the answer. Stream transports are
out of scope: their peers are real and capped per source — except DoH, which
was not, and is #170.

Four shapes were built, each against the same harness: `udp_main` over real
sockets, a mock internet, a legitimate client sending ten queries a second for
4 s under attack. Times at a tenth of production — 1 s timer, 250 ms
exchanges, 128 places, so 128 queries a second saturates — and the
legitimate zone answering in 50 or 200 ms. The adaptive attacker, a fresh
zone and four fresh silent addresses per query, is the one that matters;
percentages are of the legitimate client's queries answered.

| zone | shape | 128/s | 256/s | 1280/s | 2560/s | 6400/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 ms | drop the newest (the semaphore) | 100 | 55 | 10 | 10 | 5 |
| | A: drop the oldest | 100 | 100 | 100 | 5 | 0 |
| | B: 32 fetches per zone | 98 | 55 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| | C: skip a silent server 60 s | 100 | 55 | 10 | 5 | 8 |
| | D: oldest past 180 ms, else newest | 100 | 100 | 78 | 65 | 32 |
| 200 ms | drop the newest | 100 | 70 | 12 | 15 | 12 |
| | A | 100 | 100 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| | B | 100 | 70 | 15 | 8 | 12 |
| | C | 95 | 70 | 18 | 15 | 8 |
| | D | 100 | 100 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Against one zone and four fixed addresses, B and C answered 100% at every
rate; A and D as above.

- A, BIND's `recursive-clients`, is a cliff at places ÷ legitimate
  resolution time, and past it answers no legitimate client — the rate an
  attacker picks.
- B and C do nothing against an attacker who changes zone and address, and
  C has a cost of its own: in a first run with 250 ms exchanges, a healthy
  server answering in 500 ms was marked silent and its zone answered nothing.
- D evicts a legitimate resolution slower than the patience (the 200 ms row,
  past 180 ms) — one whose client RFC 8767 §5 says has stopped waiting.

**Closed 2026-10-05**, `287284c`: D, with RFC 8767 §5's 1.8 s client
response timer as the patience, evicted queries aborted, and
`dns_inflight_evicted_total`. At production timescale — 10 s timer, 128
places, attack at 128/s, ten times saturation — D answered 17 and 18 of 40
legitimate queries, the semaphore 0 of 40. Not the 78% above: the tenth-scale
harness scaled the patience too, which production cannot. Full, a place frees
every 1.8 s ÷ 128, about 71 a second, shared by attacker and client alike, so
about half is the ceiling at that rate. In production terms D is full
protection up to 1024 ÷ 1.8 s ≈ 569 queries a second, from ~102.
