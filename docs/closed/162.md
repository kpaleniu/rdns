### 162. A TLS handshake has no deadline, on DoT or DoH — **filed 2026-10-03, closed 2026-10-03**, **bug**, **ready-for-agent**

Found triaging #161. `tls::serve` (`tls.rs`, the `acceptor.accept` select)
and `https::serve` race the handshake against shutdown and nothing else.
`tcp::serve_one`'s idle timer starts after it. So a peer that opens TCP to
853 or 443 and sends no ClientHello, or half of one, holds a
`--max-connections` slot until it hangs up: the DoH hole #161 describes,
reachable on DoT as well, and before any HTTP. #155's "all four close an
idle connection" is wrong for both. DoQ is not affected: quinn's
`max_idle_timeout` covers the handshake. Two sites.

Read, not provoked.

**Category:** bug
**Summary:** Bound the TLS handshake on DoT and DoH by
`--connection-idle-timeout`.

**Current behavior:** A connection that never finishes its handshake holds
a slot until the peer closes it.

**Desired behavior:**

  ..)`; on expiry the connection is dropped and the slot released.
- An expiry counts as a handshake failure in
  `dns_tls_handshake_failures_total`, as other handshake failures do, and
  logs at DEBUG for the reason `tls.rs` gives.
- One helper both call, not two copies (§7).
- `--connection-idle-timeout`'s help in both daemons says it bounds the
  handshake.

- [ ] A test per listener: a raw TCP connection sending nothing, against
      `idle_timeout` of a few hundred ms, is closed by the server and the

      test's own wait, so the failure is a failure and not a hang).
- [ ] Workspace tests and clippy on Windows and Linux; fmt, doc and the §12
      grep clean.

**Out of scope:** idle after the handshake on DoH (#161).
