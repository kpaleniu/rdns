### 155. `TransportLimits` has no flag — **filed 2026-10-02, closed 2026-10-03**, **enhancement**, **ready-for-agent**

Split out of #153. All 10 production `TransportLimits` sites are
`TransportLimits::default()`: 128 concurrent connections per loop, 16
messages in flight per connection, 10 s idle, 5 s to finish a message, for
TCP, DoT, DoH and DoQ in both daemons. #30b made them one set of constants.
§14: "a limit with no flag is a limit nobody has reviewed", and every knob
wants an escape hatch and an off switch. #69's descriptor arithmetic is the
one measurement on record — 128 per loop across four loops is over 400
descriptors against a 1 024 soft limit.

**Since #153 closed (2026-10-02) the 10 sites are 2**, one
`EncryptedListeners::bind` per daemon; every TCP, DoT, DoH and DoQ loop takes
its limits from that value.

> *This was generated by AI during triage.*

**Triaged 2026-10-02**, grilled to a design (Q1-Q11).

**Missed when filed: reaching the limit is invisible.** At 128 the accept
loop has already accepted the next connection, then waits on the semaphore
holding it open and unserved, and accepts nothing more until one closes. No
counter, gauge or log line says so — §14: "Silence can be the right answer
and still needs to be visible somewhere." A triage claim that the permit
wait also delays shutdown by up to the idle timeout was **wrong and
withdrawn**: an idle connection `select!`s its length read against
`stop.wait()` and ends at once, so a full listener's permits free promptly.

**What the others ship (§4)** — every one makes the ceiling configurable:
BIND `tcp-clients` (150) and `tcp-idle-timeout` (30 s); NSD `tcp-count`,
"the maximum number of concurrent, active TCP connections by each server"
(100), and `tcp-timeout` (120 s); Unbound `incoming-num-tcp` (10 per thread)
and `tcp-idle-timeout` (30 s); Knot `tcp-max-clients` ("one half of the file
descriptor limit"), `tcp-idle-timeout` (10 s) and `tcp-io-timeout` (500 ms).
RFC 7766 §10: "It is likely to be advantageous to provide configurable
connection management options, for example: total number of TCP
connections; maximum TCP connections per source IP address or subnet; TCP
connection idle timeout; maximum DNS transactions per TCP connection;
maximum TCP connection duration." It says nothing on what a full server
does.

Decided, and rejected on the way:
- **Two settings, not four.** The connection limit and the idle timeout are
  what all four peers expose. In-flight per connection and the read timeout
  stay constants — no peer but Knot (`tcp-io-timeout`) offers either, and
  the reason goes beside them.
- **Per listener**, as now: a shared pool lets a DoH flood starve plain TCP;
  one setting per transport is eight flags.
- **Shared `[server]` keys** through `server_table!` (#63h), named
  `max-connections` and `connection-idle-timeout` (whole seconds). Not BIND's
  `tcp-*`: one of the four listeners is QUIC.
- **Defaults unchanged**, 128 and 10 s in both daemons. A resolver's clients
  and a secondary's are different populations (#30e), but changing a default
  is a behavior change for every deployment and wants its own measurement.
- **Refused below 1** at startup, naming the flag or key. Zero connections
  blocks every accept; a zero idle timeout fails the first length read.
  §14's floor was for a runtime value whose zero looked harmless; this one is
  checked before anything binds, which §15 prefers. "No limit" for zero was
  declined because it reopens the descriptor exhaustion the limit exists for
  — so these two knobs have no off switch, against §14, and that is why.
- **Wait for a slot before accepting**, so the waiting connection stays in
  the kernel's backlog and holds no descriptor. Accept-and-close was
  declined: it changes what clients see and nothing asks for it.
- **Visibility:** a gauge `dns_connections_open` and a counter
  `dns_connection_accept_waits_total`, each labelled `transport` (`tcp`,
  `dot`, `doh`, `doq`), updated where a slot is taken and released, never
  sampled at scrape (§14); and one warning when a listener becomes full and
  one info line when it has room again, once per episode. Labelled rather
  than eight names: `metrics.rs` already has labelled families, and one
  alert rule covers four listeners.
- RFC 7766 §10's other three items are **#156**.

No glossary term, no ADR: a setting can be reversed.

#### Agent brief

**Category:** enhancement
**Summary:** Make the stream listeners' connection ceiling and idle timeout
settable, and make reaching the ceiling visible.

**Current behavior:** TCP, DoT, DoH and DoQ each allow 128 concurrent
connections and close a connection idle for 10 s, from
`TransportLimits::default()` with no flag or key. A full listener accepts
the next connection, holds it unserved until a slot frees, and reports
nothing.

**Desired behavior:**
- Both daemons take `--max-connections` and `--connection-idle-timeout`
  (seconds), and `[server]` keys `max-connections` and
  `connection-idle-timeout`, as shared `server_table!` keys with defaults
  128 and 10. A value below 1 is refused at startup naming the flag or key;
  `--check-config` reaches the refusal. Help text says the connection limit
  applies to each listener.
- `TransportLimits` is built from them and reaches every stream listener
  through #153's bound value. In-flight per connection and the read timeout
  stay constants, each with its reason beside it.
- An accept loop takes a slot before accepting, so a full listener holds no
  connection it is not serving.
- `dns_connections_open{transport}` and
  `dns_connection_accept_waits_total{transport}` appear in the scrape for
  every configured stream listener, updated where a slot is taken and
  released. A listener logs one warning on becoming full and one info line
  on having room again.

**Key interfaces:**
- `TransportLimits`: two fields come from configuration; the shape stays.
- The shared `[server]` table and each daemon's `default_*` functions.
- `DnsMetrics`: a labelled gauge and a labelled counter.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] With `--max-connections 2`, a third connection to a listener is not
      accepted while two are open; it is served once one closes; the wait
      counter for that transport has moved and the gauge read 2.
- [ ] With `--connection-idle-timeout 1`, an idle connection is closed
      after about a second.
- [ ] `0` for either is refused by `--check-config` with the setting named,
      on the flag and in the file.
- [ ] Exactly one warning and one info line for a full-then-free episode,
      however many connections waited.
- [ ] The two settings are in both daemons through `server_table!`'s
      declarations and projection (#103), not assigned by hand.
- [ ] Workspace tests and clippy on Windows and Linux; fmt, doc and the §12
      grep clean.

**Out of scope:**
- Per-source connection limits, transactions per connection, connection
  duration (#156).
- The UDP side, and any default's value.

**Closed 2026-10-03**, `05d4668`. Every box ticked as written, with two
corrections to the row:

- **The row said all four listeners close an idle connection after 10 s.
  DoH never did** — hyper is given no timer, and its HTTP/2 server has no
  idle close. Provoked at 200 ms with a throwaway test: an HTTP/1.1 and an h2
  connection both still open after 3 s. Filed as **#161**;
  `--connection-idle-timeout`'s help says it does not apply to DoH.
- The refusal of `0` is the type, not a check: `NonZeroUsize` and
  `NonZeroU64`, so clap names the flag and serde the key with its line
  number, both above the dry-run exit (§17).

The slot is taken before `accept` in all four loops
(`rdns_transport::slots`); the gauge moves when a connection takes the slot,
not when the loop does, so an idle listener reads 0 and not 1. The episode
ends when a release leaves a permit free, which a release to a queued waiter
does not — so a flood is one WARN and one INFO, measured by
`a_full_listener_waits_for_a_slot_and_says_so_once` and failing with two
WARNs without the flag. Accepting and then waiting, as before, is invisible to
a client, so that half has no failing-first test.

`an_idle_connection_is_closed_after_the_configured_timeout` runs `rdnsd` as a
process — the first test in `rdnsd/tests/startup.rs` to bind a socket — and
fails at its 8 s guard with the default limits passed instead. 1396 tests on
Windows, 1417 on Linux.
